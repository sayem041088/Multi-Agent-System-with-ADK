PART 1: PLOT OUTLINE

TITLE: POETICAL SCIENCE

LOGLINE:
In Victorian London, mathematical prodigy Ada Lovelace—haunted by the scandalous legacy of her estranged poet father, Lord Byron—collaborates with eccentric inventor Charles Babbage on a mechanical calculating machine, defying societal constraints and debilitating illness to author the world’s first computer program and foresee the modern digital age.

CHARACTERS & THEMES:
- Ada Lovelace (Augusta Ada Byron): Fiercely intelligent, visionary, torn between her mother’s demands for pure logic and her father’s inherited poetic imagination.
- Charles Babbage: Brilliant, obsessive, yet politically stubborn inventor of the Difference and Analytical Engines.
- Lady Anne Isabella Byron: Ada’s cold, overprotective mother, terrified that any creative impulse in Ada will lead to Byron's "madness."
- Mary Somerville: Renowned polymath and Ada’s supportive mentor who champions women in science.
- William King (Lord Lovelace): Ada’s supportive yet socially constrained husband.
- Major Themes: The harmony of art and science ("poetical science"), the pursuit of legacy against physical mortality, female intellect within Victorian patriarchy.

THREE-ACT PLOT OUTLINE:

ACT I: THE CAGED BIRD
- Opening / Childhood: Young Ada is confined to strict regimens of mathematics and science by Lady Byron, designed systematically to stamp out any romantic "poetic madness" inherited from her father, Lord Byron. Ada secretly sketches flying machines ("Flyology"), blending mechanistic design with boundless imagination.
- Inciting Incident: At age seventeen, introduced into society by her mentor, scientist Mary Somerville, Ada attends a London salon hosted by Charles Babbage. While others view Babbage's brass prototype, the Difference Engine, as a mere parlor trick or glorified clock, Ada perceives the profound mathematical logic pulsing beneath its gears.
- Debate: Lady Byron urges Ada to settle into marriage and abandon frivolous obsessions. Babbage, cynical after years of government skepticism, dismisses Ada as another aristocratic admirer. But Ada solves a complex mathematical problem Babbage assumed no salon guest could grasp, proving her intellect.
- Act I Break: Ada marries William King, securing social independence, but makes a vow to herself: she will not be merely a high-society wife; she will bridge her father's poetic intuition with mathematical truth.

ACT II: THE WEAVING OF SYMBOLS
- Act II-A (Rising Action): 
  - Ada reunites with Babbage, who has abandoned the simpler Difference Engine for an audacious new dream: the "Analytical Engine," driven by Jacquard punch cards.
  - Ada balances motherhood and high-society obligations with intensive study under logic masters. While Babbage focuses purely on number-crunching calculations, Ada realizes the revolutionary leap: if the machine can manipulate arbitrary symbols according to rules, it can process music, text, and abstract thought.
  - Babbage struggles with political adversaries in Parliament, who cut his funding. Desperate to rally international support, Babbage lectures in Turin, prompting Italian mathematician Luigi Menabrea to write a French treatise on the Analytical Engine.
- Midpoint: 
  - Ada proposes translating Menabrea's paper into English. Babbage enthusiastically agrees, asking: "Why not add notes of your own?" 
  - Ada seizes the challenge, realizing this is her chance to reveal the machine's true potential. Her notes expand exponentially, becoming three times the length of the original text.
- Act II-B (Complications & Conflict):
  - Ada works relentlessly on "Note G," meticulously designing a stepwise routine to instruct the machine to calculate Bernoulli numbers—the world's first true algorithmic computer program.
  - Her health deteriorates rapidly with chronic pain and exhaustion; she battles severe illness and the side effects of Victorian medicines (laudanum).
  - Tensions explode when Babbage tries to insert an angry, politically damaging preface attacking the British government into her paper. Ada refuses to let his bitter political vendetta taint her life's work. In a fiery confrontation, she asserts her ownership over the treatise and demands he respect her intellectual sovereignty.

ACT III: THE ETERNAL SPARK
- Climax:
  - Ada stands her ground. The work is published in 1843 under the initials "A.A.L." to protect it from gender bias. Her treatise captivates prominent thinkers, including Michael Faraday.
  - In Note A and Note G, her words ring across history: "The Analytical Engine weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard loom weaves flowers and leaves." She proves that machines can act as extensions of human creativity, establishing the foundational philosophy of modern computing.
- Falling Action:
  - As uterine cancer claims her physical strength at age 36, Ada finds inner peace. She has reconciled the mathematical discipline enforced by her mother with the transcendent creative passion of her father, christening her methodology "Poetical Science."
  - Babbage visits her bedside, humbly acknowledging her as the true visionary who saw the soul within his machine.
- Resolution:
  - Ada requests to be buried beside the father she never knew, having completed his legacy not with verse, but with algorithms.
  - Final visual: Ada's handwritten algorithmic punch cards and flow diagrams dissolve into the glowing fiber-optic and algorithmic data streams of the 21st century. Closing titles state that over a century later, her notes inspired Alan Turing, and she remains celebrated as the visionary mother of computer programming.

# BOX OFFICE POTENTIAL REPORT: *POETICAL SCIENCE*

**Project Overview:**  
* **Title:** *Poetical Science*  
* **Genre:** Period Biographical Drama / STEM Prestige Feature  
* **Subject:** Ada Lovelace & Charles Babbage  
* **Target Rating:** PG-13  
* **Ideal Distribution Partner:** Searchlight Pictures, Focus Features, A24, or Sony Pictures Classics  

---

## 1. Executive Summary

*Poetical Science* occupies a commercially proven theatrical subgenre: the **cerebral British period biopic centered on a scientific or mathematical visionary**. 

The historical precedent for films depicting the origins of computing and scientific breakthrough—notably *The Imitation Game* and *The Theory of Everything*—demonstrates that adult audiences turn out in substantial numbers when technical achievement is anchored by high emotional and personal stakes. Furthermore, the modern cultural resonance of female trailblazers in STEM (evidenced by *Hidden Figures*) gives *Poetical Science* significant crossover appeal beyond the traditional arthouse demographic.

However, historical dramas in the post-2020 theatrical landscape carry heightened risk. To achieve financial profitability, *Poetical Science* must be budgeted responsibly ($25M–$35M) and positioned as an autumn prestige/awards-season contender.

---

## 2. Box Office Comps & Precedents

To evaluate the revenue potential of *Poetical Science*, we analyze three primary tiers of comparable releases:

### A. The Golden Standards (Direct Genre Precedents)
* **The Imitation Game (2014)**  
  * *Global Box Office:* **$233.5M** (Domestic: $91.1M / International: $142.4M)  
  * *Production Budget:* $14M  
  * *Relevance:* The definitive comp. Centered on Alan Turing and the birth of computational logic, wartime tragedy, social isolation, and visionary genius. Proves that mathematical/algorithmic narrative pivots can be translated into major commercial box office hits when framed with emotional urgency and top-tier acting talent.
* **Hidden Figures (2016)**  
  * *Global Box Office:* **$236.2M** (Domestic: $169.6M / International: $66.6M)  
  * *Production Budget:* $25M  
  * *Relevance:* Demonstrated an immense, often underserved theatrical appetite for female mathematical pioneers overcoming institutional and societal resistance.
* **The Theory of Everything (2014)**  
  * *Global Box Office:* **$123.7M** (Domestic: $35.9M / International: $87.8M)  
  * *Production Budget:* $15M  
  * *Relevance:* Examines the collision of physical illness, romantic devotion, and theoretical physics in high-society England, yielding exceptional international returns.

### B. Recent Period & Literary/Inventor Dramas (Mid-Range & Cautionary Comps)
* **Little Women (2019)**  
  * *Global Box Office:* **$218.8M** on a $40M budget. Demonstrated strong multi-generational female turnout for period pieces with intellectual, non-conformist protagonists.
* **The Current War (2017/2019)**  
  * *Global Box Office:* **$12.3M** on a $30M budget. Suffered from an overly clinical, pacing-challenged focus on mechanical patents without sufficient emotional resonance.
* **Radioactive (2019/2020)**  
  * *Global Box Office:* **~$4.0M** (severely disrupted by the pandemic/streaming pivot). Highlighted the risk of Marie Curie's story becoming too fractured or cold, serving as a cautionary tale against overly episodic bio-narratives.
* **Emily (2022)**  
  * *Global Box Office:* **~$4.5M** (specialty limited run). Showcased the floor for a moody, 19th-century female literary biopic that remains confined purely to the niche arthouse circuit.

---

## 3. Audience Demographics & Market Drivers

### Core Audience Segments
1. **The Prestige / Adult Drama Demographic (Ages 35–65+):** Frequent consumers of historical drama, period pieces, and award-season releases (the core *Downton Abbey* / Focus Features audience).
2. **Young Adult & Millennial Women (Ages 18–34):** Drawn to feminist revisionist history, literary adaptations, and stories of women claiming intellectual sovereignty (comparable to the demographic driver for *Little Women*).
3. **The Global Tech / Academic Demographic:** Engineers, coders, educators, and STEM affinity organizations representing a high-affinity grassroots audience with institutional group-sales potential.

### Market Drivers
* **Timeliness of the AI / Computing Narrative:** In the current cultural conversation surrounding computing, artificial intelligence, and algorithmic culture, a film exploring the philosophical genesis of software ("Note G" and "Poetical Science") holds urgent thematic relevance.
* **Lord Byron / Romantic Aura:** The dynamic tension between Lord Byron’s romantic legacy and Lady Byron’s mathematical rigor elevates the film from a dry scientific chronicle to an evocative psychological gothic drama.

---

## 4. Financial Projections & Scenarios

Assuming a controlled **production budget of $28M – $32M** and an awards-qualifying platform release expanding wide in November/December:

| Metric | Bear Case (Niche Arthouse) | Base Case (Prestige Contender) | Bull Case (Awards Breakout) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Domestic Box Office (NA)** | $12M – $18M | $40M – $60M | $85M – $115M |
| **International Box Office** | $15M – $22M | $55M – $75M | $95M – $135M |
| **Worldwide Theatrical Total** | **$27M – $40M** | **$95M – $135M** | **$180M – $250M** |

* **Bear Case ($27M–$40M WW):** The film lands with modest critical enthusiasm and fails to secure major Academy Award nominations. It plays primarily as a niche British costume drama, requiring domestic PVOD and SVOD streaming output deals to break even.
* **Base Case ($95M–$135M WW):** The film opens on the festival circuit (Telluride/TIFF), receives glowing reviews for its lead actress, earns key nominations (Best Actress, Production Design, Costume Design), and mirrors the run of *The Theory of Everything*. Highly profitable.
* **Bull Case ($180M–$250M WW):** Crossover cultural phenomenon. Captures the high-water mark of *The Imitation Game* and *Hidden Figures*, buoyed by universal critical acclaim, educational tie-ins, and multi-quadrant adult appeal.

---

## 5. Strategic Recommendations for Maximizing Commercial Viability

1. **Star Casting for the Two Leads:**  
   * **Ada Lovelace:** Requires an actress who blends intellectual intensity with vulnerability and box-office cachet (e.g., Florence Pugh, Anya Taylor-Joy, Saoirse Ronan, or Daisy Edgar-Jones).  
   * **Charles Babbage:** An eccentric, magnetic veteran British presence (e.g., Benedict Cumberbatch, Andrew Scott, Colin Firth, or Mark Rylance) is vital to make the intellectual sparring compelling.
2. **Cinematic Treatment of "Poetical Science":**  
   * Avoid visual cliches of chalkboard mathematics. Translate Ada's visualization of punch cards and "weaving algebraical patterns" into cinematic, lyrical VFX motifs (analogous to the visual style of *The Queen’s Gambit* or *A Beautiful Mind*).
3. **Optimized Release Strategy:**  
   * Debut at September film festivals (Venice, Telluride, or TIFF) to generate critical momentum.
   * Roll out via limited platform release in early November in New York/London/Los Angeles, expanding wide by Thanksgiving or Christmas to capitalize on word-of-mouth and holiday adult moviegoing.
4. **Ancillary & Post-Theatrical Value:**  
   * Biopics of historical pioneers enjoy exceptionally high long-tail retention on SVOD platforms, international broadcast syndication, and high educational licensing revenues.

PART 3: CASTING REPORT
================================================================================

Here are casting suggestions for the key roles in ***Poetical Science***, featuring actors whose acclaimed past performances demonstrate the exact dramatic nuance, period authenticity, and emotional range required for each character:

---

### 1. Ada Lovelace (Augusta Ada Byron)
*Fiercely intelligent, visionary, and passionate; torn between the emotional tempest of her father’s romantic legacy and her mother’s demands for pure cold logic.*

* **Top Choice: Saoirse Ronan**
  * **Why She Fits:** Ronan excels at playing fiercely independent, intellectually voracious young women who push against societal boundaries (*Little Women*, *Atonement*, *Lady Bird*). Her Oscar-nominated portrayal of Jo March showed her unique ability to project both relentless creative fire and quiet vulnerability. She has the classical period-drama pedigree and the emotional depth to convey Ada’s intellectual breakthrough and tragic physical decline.
* **Alternative: Florence Pugh**
  * **Why She Fits:** Pugh brings an earthy, electrifying vitality and stubborn intelligence to period roles (*Little Women*, *The Wonder*, *Oppenheimer*). Her performance in *The Wonder* demonstrated her mastery of Victorian nuance, grief, and intellectual resolve against rigid dogma.
* **Alternative: Jessie Buckley**
  * **Why She Fits:** Known for her raw, poetic intensity and emotional transparency (*The Lost Daughter*, *Women Talking*, *Wild Rose*), Buckley would capture the feverish, Byron-esque creative obsession that fuels Ada's concept of "Poetical Science."

---

### 2. Charles Babbage
*Brilliant, eccentric, cantankerous, and obsessively stubborn; a frustrated visionary whose brass engines consume his life.*

* **Top Choice: Jared Harris**
  * **Why He Fits:** Harris has garnered massive critical acclaim for playing complex, obsessive, and tragic intellectual figures (*Chernobyl*, *The Terror*, *Mad Men*, *Lincoln*). In *Chernobyl* (Valery Legasov), he portrayed a brilliant scientist colliding head-on with political bureaucracy and stubborn pride—a dynamic identical to Babbage’s fraught relationship with the British government and his fiery debates with Ada.
* **Alternative: Paul Giamatti**
  * **Why He Fits:** Giamatti (*The Holdovers*, *John Adams*) is a master of the cantankerous, brilliant curmudgeon who masks deep insecurity and high ideals behind a barbed exterior. He would bring incredible sparks and pathos to Babbage’s exasperation and final humble acknowledgment of Ada.
* **Alternative: Mark Rylance**
  * **Why He Fits:** Rylance (*Bridge of Spies*, *Wolf Hall*, *Don't Look Up*) brings an eccentric, quiet eccentricity and deep intellect to the screen. He could emphasize Babbage’s mechanical genius and tragic inability to see the broader poetic picture that Ada grasps.

---

### 3. Lady Anne Isabella Byron
*Ada’s severe, aristocratic mother; governed by cold rationality and religious/moral rectitude, terrified of Lord Byron’s “madness” taking root in her daughter.*

* **Top Choice: Kristin Scott Thomas**
  * **Why She Fits:** Scott Thomas is peerless when portraying aristocratic frostiness layered over hidden emotional trauma (*Darkest Hour*, *The English Patient*, *Nowhere Boy*). In *Nowhere Boy*, she played John Lennon’s stern, repressive Aunt Mimi to great critical acclaim, demonstrating the exact balance of overprotective maternal control and repressed fear needed for Lady Byron.
* **Alternative: Emily Watson**
  * **Why She Fits:** Watson (*Chernobyl*, *Gosford Park*, *Oranges and Sunshine*) brings severe dignity infused with underlying maternal panic. She would humanize Lady Byron's harshness so audiences understand her actions stem from genuine terror rather than simple cruelty.

---

### 4. Mary Somerville
*The pioneering polymath, astronomer, and intellectual trailblazer; warm, authoritative, and Ada’s first champion in the male-dominated scientific salon.*

* **Top Choice: Emma Thompson**
  * **Why She Fits:** Thompson embodies natural intellectual authority, wit, and maternal warmth (*Sense and Sensibility*, *Wit*, *Howards End*). Audiences immediately believe her as a giant of 19th-century science who commands respect in any room while serving as an inspiring, nurturing mentor to young Ada.
* **Alternative: Harriet Walter**
  * **Why She Fits:** A master of period pieces (*Sense and Sensibility*, *The Crown*, *Succession*), Walter possesses a sharp, aristocratic bearing and natural scientific gravitas that would make Mary Somerville feel formidable yet deeply supportive.

---

### 5. William King (Lord Lovelace)
*Ada’s husband; an aristocratic peer who genuinely admires Ada and provides her legal cover and social standing, but remains bound by the rigid social codes and expectations of his station.*

* **Top Choice: Matthew Goode**
  * **Why He Fits:** Goode has repeatedly earned praise for playing refined, empathetic, yet socially bound gentlemen (*The Imitation Game*, *Downton Abbey*, *The Crown*). He knows how to support a brilliant female lead on screen without fading into the background, balancing genuine affection with Victorian aristocratic reserve.
* **Alternative: Dan Stevens**
  * **Why He Fits:** Stevens (*Downton Abbey*, *Sense & Sensibility*, *I'm Your Man*) can balance high-society polish with genuine intellectual curiosity, making William an active, sympathetic partner rather than a purely passive bystander.
