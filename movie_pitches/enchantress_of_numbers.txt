TITLE: ENCHANTRESS OF NUMBERS

LOGLINE:
Torn between her mother’s strict mathematical discipline and the volatile poetic legacy of her estranged father Lord Byron, Ada Lovelace forms an extraordinary intellectual partnership with eccentric inventor Charles Babbage—defying Victorian society to conceptualize the world’s first computer algorithm and foresee the digital dawn.

PLOT OUTLINE:

ACT I: THE ARCHITECTURE OF DREAMS
- Setup: England, 1828–1833. Young Ada Byron grows up under the hyper-vigilant eye of her mother, Lady Anne Isabella Milbanke, who enforces a ruthless curriculum of mathematics and logic to purge any trace of Lord Byron’s "dangerous poetical madness." Ada, however, blends both inheritances: she designs mechanical flying machines driven by steam, seeing an innate lyrical harmony inside mechanical formulas.
- Inciting Incident: Introduced to the intellectual vanguard by her mentor, scientist Mary Somerville, 17-year-old Ada attends one of Charles Babbage’s fashionable London salons. Amid the skepticism of London's elite, Ada sees Babbage demonstrate a prototype of his brass Difference Engine. While onlookers see only a novelty calculator, Ada experiences an epiphany: numbers can represent symbols, ideas, and reality itself.
- Plot Point One: Babbage recognizes in Ada an intellect unlike any other. When Ada enters society and marries William King (who becomes Earl of Lovelace), she makes an audacious pact with her new husband and Babbage: she will fulfill her aristocratic duties as Countess and mother, but she will never abandon her true calling as an "Analyst and Metaphysician."

ACT II: POETICAL SCIENCE
- Act II-A: Balancing three young children with intense intellectual study, Ada delves into Babbage's newly conceived, far more ambitious creation: the Analytical Engine, a theoretical steam-powered general-purpose computer. As the British government revokes Babbage’s funding in frustration over delays and expense, Babbage turns bitter and combative. Ada steps into the breach, realizing that the world does not comprehend what Babbage has designed.
- Midpoint: In 1842, Swiss-Italian engineer Luigi Menabrea publishes a dry, academic treatise on the Analytical Engine in French. Ada proposes not only translating the text into English but augmenting it with her own explanatory "Notes." Babbage enthusiastically agrees. Working in a fever of intellectual liberation, Ada conceives "Poetical Science"—the fusion of imagination and calculation. She posits that the engine could weave algebraic patterns just as the Jacquard loom weaves silk, and predicts that one day machines could compose music, create art, and alter human civilization.
- Act II-B: As Ada drafts her annotations, physical exhaustion and recurring chronic pain set in. Victorian conventions push back against an aristocratic woman publishing technical treatises; Lady Byron attempts to intervene and reassert moral control over Ada's passionate obsession. In private correspondence, Babbage tries to assert authorial primacy over the paper, sparking a fierce clash of egos. Ada refuses to be diminished or treated as an amanuensis, demanding full recognition as co-architect of the concept.
- Low Point / All Hope Is Lost: Ada finishes "Note G"—a complete, step-by-step algorithm to calculate Bernoulli numbers, the first computer program in history. However, upon publication (signed discreetly as "A.A.L."), the scientific establishment remains cool and uncomprehending. Babbage’s funds are entirely exhausted, and the machine remains unbuilt. In financial desperation to self-fund the engine, Ada and a syndicate attempt to apply mathematical probability models to horse racing, leading to heavy losses, scandal, and strained family relations. Soon after, Ada is diagnosed with terminal uterine cancer at age 35.

ACT III: THE ETERNAL CODE
- The Climax: Confined to her bed and racing against the same age at which her father died (36), Ada reconciles her divided soul. She realizes that her mother’s logic and her father’s romantic fervor were never opposites, but the necessary twin forces that allowed her to see into the distant future. Defying her physical agony, Ada dictates revisions, secures her correspondence, and shares a poignant reconciliation with Babbage, passing him the mantle to safeguard their shared dream.
- Resolution: Surrounded by her books, papers, and loved ones, Ada finds peace knowing her thoughts are etched into history, indestructible even if the physical brass gears were never cast.
- Epilogue / Visual Coda: The mechanical ticking and whirring of Babbage's brass gears seamlessly transitions into the pulse of electronic circuitry, microprocessors, and fiber optics of the modern internet. A concluding title card honors Ada Lovelace as the mother of computer programming, whose vision of "Poetical Science" anticipated our digital world over a century before it came to life.
